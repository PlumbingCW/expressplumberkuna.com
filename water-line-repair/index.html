A water service line is the buried pipe that carries water from the meter to your house, and when it fails the signs are hard to miss. Kuna Express Plumbing answers the line 24/7 for emergency water service line repair in Kuna, ID.

If water is flowing from the ground or your house has lost water, close the main valve inside the house and call. A plumber confirms where the line failed, explains the repair choices and quotes the price before work starts.

Signs the buried line has a problem

Water lines hide underground, so the symptoms show up in the yard, at the meter and at your faucets. Any of these is worth a call.

  • A wet or soggy patch in the yard when it has not rained and the sprinklers are off.
  • Water bubbling up from the soil or collecting in the meter box.
  • A meter that keeps turning with every fixture in the house shut off.
  • Pressure that has dropped across the whole house, not just one faucet.
  • No water at all, or water that sputters and surges.
  • A water bill that climbs with no visible leak inside.

A single symptom does not prove the line is the problem. A plumber confirms it with a pressure test and by checking the main valve, the meter and the shutoffs before anyone digs.

Whose pipe is it: the city's or yours

This question decides who gets the first call, so it is worth a straight answer. In many water systems the utility looks after the main in the street and the meter, and the owner is responsible for the line from there to the house. The details differ from one system to the next, so confirm with the City of Kuna Water Department before assuming either way.

If water is running from the meter itself or from the street side, call the City of Kuna water emergency line at (208) 573-7676. The city says its Water Department staff are on call 24/7 for water emergencies. If the leak is on the house side of the meter, it is normally the owner's repair, and that is where we come in.

No water or low pressure at the house

Low pressure everywhere in the house is a different problem from one weak faucet. Before blaming the buried line, check whether neighbors have the same trouble, whether the main valve is fully open and whether a water softener or filter bypass is closed. If a single faucet is weak, an aerator or cartridge is usually the cause.

When pressure drops everywhere, a service line leak, a partly closed valve or a failing pressure regulator are the usual causes. No water at all after a repair or a dig can mean a shutoff was left closed. If none of those match, the line itself is the next place to look, and a plumber tests it.

Before anyone digs

Safe steps before and during a dig

Close the main valve inside the house. Stay back from water that is shooting out of the ground and keep children and pets away from soft, wet soil. Do not dig on your own until buried utilities have been marked, because a shovel through a gas or electric line is far worse than a leaking water line.

In Kuna, the Dig Line number is (208) 342-1585, and calling it before digging is the standard first step. Marking takes time, so tell dispatch if the line is flooding the property and a plumber can shut off the supply and plan the dig around the marks.

Permits, licensing and digging

The City of Kuna plumbing permit application lists water lines among its covered work. The Building Department at (208) 922-5546 can tell you whether your repair or replacement needs a permit. Work without a permit incurs double permit fees as the minimum charge, so asking first is cheaper than finding out later.

Idaho licenses plumbers through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses, and you can check any plumber's license through the state's online search. If a homeowner pulls a permit for their own primary or secondary residence, the state says that is allowed, but not for commercial buildings.

Wells and long runs of buried pipe

Homes outside city water often have a line from a private well to the house, sometimes with a long run to a barn or outbuilding. The same signs apply: a wet patch, pressure that cycles or a pump that runs constantly. A plumber can find a leak on the house side of the well, while the well, pump and tank are usually the job of a well contractor.

Central District Health recommends testing private well water once a year for bacteria and nitrates, since harmful levels often cannot be seen, smelled or tasted. After any repair to a line that serves a well, a test is a sensible step, and the health district's line is (208) 375-5211.

Old and new service lines in Kuna

Kuna's population grew from 15,210 in 2010 to 24,011 in 2020, so many service lines in newer neighborhoods are comparatively young. A young line can still fail from a poor joint, a rock against the pipe or damage during landscaping. Age alone is not the question, and a plumber looks at the material and the failure before suggesting how much to replace.

What to do if the line fails

Close the main valve at the house if you can reach it. Do not dig near the line before Dig Line (208) 342-1585 has marked utilities. Keep children and pets away from soft, soaked ground, and photograph the wet area for the plumber.
