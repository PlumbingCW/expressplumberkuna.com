Stop the water: your first five minutes

Every household member should know where the main valve is before a pipe ever fails. These steps work for most homes.

  1. Find the main shutoff. It is often where the supply line enters the house, in a garage, a utility room, a crawlspace or by the water heater.
  2. Turn it fully clockwise, or a quarter turn if it has a lever handle.
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to relieve pressure and let the pipes drain.
  4. Turn off the water heater power or gas control so the tank does not run dry and overheat.
  5. Call dispatch and tell them where the water is and whether the shutoff worked.

If the valve will not turn or the water does not slow, say so on the call. A stuck main valve is a repair job of its own, and our faucet repair page covers the smaller shutoffs under sinks and behind toilets that you can use to isolate a single fixture.

Before you touch anything electrical

Water and electricity do not mix. If water is dripping near outlets, a panel, a light fixture or an appliance, stay out of the wet area. Turn off the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If you cannot, leave the room and tell dispatch.

What counts as a burst pipe emergency

A small drip can wait for a scheduled visit. These cannot.

  • Water is spraying or running steadily from a pipe, fitting or wall.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling or flooding a garage or crawlspace.
  • The main valve will not close.
  • You lost water to the whole house because a line failed.
  • Water is reaching outlets, a breaker panel or a heater.

In any of these cases, shut off what you can and call right away. If the pipe is under a slab or inside a wall and you cannot see it, the water still has to be stopped at the main, and a plumber will find the failed section.

Frozen pipes in Kuna winters

Pipes that freeze can split, and the damage often shows up only after a thaw. National Weather Service data cited by KBOI puts Boise's average first freeze in mid-October. Kuna is about 18 miles southwest of downtown Boise, so a good rule is to treat mid-October as the time to prepare in the Boise area instead of waiting for the first cold snap.

Disconnect garden hoses, shut off and drain outdoor lines where possible, and insulate exposed pipes in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls. If a pipe is frozen but not yet split, open the faucet, warm the area slowly with a hair dryer and do not use an open flame. If you find a split or water starts flowing as it thaws, shut off the main and call.

Why pipes burst when it is not freezing

Not every burst pipe comes from cold. Old galvanized steel rusts from the inside until a thin spot gives way, and copper can develop pinholes. Plastic fittings can crack at a joint, and high water pressure speeds up all of these failures.

Low pressure or discolored water after a repair

After a repair, run the cold taps for a few minutes to flush out air and any loose debris. A little sputtering at first is normal because air is trapped in the line. Brown or cloudy water can come from rust and sediment that were shaken loose when the water was shut off and restarted.

If pressure stays weak after the repair, check whether aerators on faucets are clogged with debris and whether the main valve is fully open. If neither fixes it, call us, because it can mean another leak or a partly closed valve. Low pressure and no water at all are common signs of a buried line problem, which we cover on our water line repair page.

Cleanup, drywall and insurance

Once the water is off and the pipe is repaired, drying matters as much as the repair. Open windows, run fans, take photos of every damaged surface and call your insurance company early. Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold if left, so removing soaked materials sooner is better.

We repair the plumbing. Drying services, drywall and painting are separate trades, and we are happy to describe what we found so those crews know where the leak was.
