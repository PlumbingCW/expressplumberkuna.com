Symptoms that point to a tankless problem

Tankless owners rarely say the heater is broken. They describe what the water is doing.

  • The water runs lukewarm or swings between hot and cold during a shower.
  • The unit shows an error code, beeps or shuts off partway through use.
  • Hot water takes a long time to arrive, or only comes out at a weak trickle.
  • There is a smell of burning, or the vent area looks scorched or sooty.
  • Water is dripping from the unit or the connections beneath it.
  • The unit runs fine in winter but struggles when the whole house uses water at once.

Write down any code before you call. If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and Intermountain Gas first.

Why flush and descale matters in Kuna

The City of Kuna's water quality report says all of southern Idaho has hard water because of naturally occurring calcium. A tankless heater runs water through a small heat exchanger at high temperature, and that is exactly where scale builds up. As the passages narrow, flow drops, the unit works harder and error codes appear.

A flush circulates a descaling solution through the heat exchanger and rinses it out. The right interval depends on your water and how often the unit runs, so we do not give a one-size number.

If you have never flushed it, the age of the unit does not rule out a flush, but a plumber should look at it first to see if the exchanger is worth treating. Our water softener installation page explains testing your tap if scale keeps coming back.

What a flush and tune-up visit covers

1

Check the symptoms

The plumber reviews the error history and runs the unit to see how it behaves.

2

Isolate and circulate

The unit's isolation valves are closed and the heat exchanger is circulated with a descaling solution.

3

Clean the filters and inlets

Water and gas inlet screens are cleaned, since debris there mimics scale.

4

Test the flow and temperature

We run hot taps, watch the temperature rise and confirm the unit fires and shuts off properly.

Units installed without service valves can still be flushed, but valves need to be added first. Ask dispatch if you are not sure the unit has them.

Error codes and repairs

A code is a message from the unit's control board, and the meaning differs by maker. Common causes include scale, a clogged inlet screen, a failed flow sensor, a vent problem or an ignition fault. A plumber reads the code, checks the likely cause and tells you whether the repair is worth doing.

Before you call about a code

Note the brand, the model number on the data plate and the exact code. Turn the unit off and on once, and do not keep resetting it if the code returns right away. Never open the cabinet of a gas unit yourself. If the unit is leaking, close its cold water inlet valve and switch off the power.

Burnt sensor wires, a stuck relay or a failed board are repairs a plumber can diagnose. Some older models may no longer have parts available. When that happens we say so, and show you the replacement price next to the repair price.

A tankless unit heats water as it flows, so it can only raise the temperature by a fixed amount at a given flow. In cold ground-water months that limit shows up if you run two showers and a dishwasher together. A plumber sizes the unit to your household, not to a catalog.

Gas, power and venting honesty

Switching to tankless is not always a simple swap. Gas models often need a larger gas line than a tank heater, a new vent and a condensate drain. Electric models can ask for more from the electrical panel than the house has to spare. Per the City of Kuna, Idaho Power provides electricity and Intermountain Gas provides natural gas, so those utilities may need to be involved in a larger load.

We tell you when a tankless upgrade does not make sense for your house. Sometimes a tank or a hybrid heater is the better fit, and our water heater installation page compares the options.
