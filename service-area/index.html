Kuna, ID

Kuna is our home base, with ZIP code 83634. Calls inside Kuna come first. We serve the center of town, the Indian Creek corridor and the N Meridian Road side of the city.

The City of Kuna runs its drinking water from city wells and operates its own sewer mains, so a problem at the street goes to the city and a problem inside your property goes to a plumber. See drain cleaning and water heater repair for our most common Kuna calls.

What Kuna conditions mean for your plumbing

The City of Kuna says all of southern Idaho has hard water, so scale on fixtures and water heaters is a regular reason for calls. Our water softener installation page covers testing your tap. Irrigation season in Kuna generally starts in mid-to-late April, which is when sprinkler line leaks and backflow assemblies show up in service calls.

Kuna grew from 15,210 residents in 2010 to 24,011 in 2020, so many homes have younger plumbing, and some lots near the original townsite have older lines. A plumber checks the pipe material on the first visit and tells you what you have.

What to have ready

Give dispatch the street address, the nearest cross street and any gate, dog or access notes. If the water is in the house, close the main shutoff first. More on the shutoff steps is on our burst pipe repair page.

How dispatch decides who goes where

Plumbers are not parked in every town. Dispatch looks at where crews are, how urgent each call is and how far the address sits from Kuna. A flooding basement or a sewage backup moves ahead of a slow drain. A planned job such as a new fixture is booked for a day that fits your calendar and the plumber's route.

That is why we ask for a full address and cross streets on the first call, and why we do not promise an arrival time. We would rather tell you the truth about the schedule than a number that traffic or another emergency can break.

Seasonal calls around Kuna

Spring brings irrigation startup, so sprinkler line leaks and backflow questions rise. Cold snaps bring frozen and split pipes, and the average first freeze in the Boise area falls in mid-October according to National Weather Service data cited by KBOI. Summer and fall bring water heater and drain calls that have nothing to do with the weather. The line is open for all of them.
