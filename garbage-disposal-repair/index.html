Disposal emergencies: when to call right away

A disposal problem becomes urgent when water or power is involved. These are the cases that should not wait.

  • Water is dripping or running from the body of the unit and filling the cabinet.
  • The sink is full of standing water and will not drain.
  • The disposal is smoking, smells of burning or trips the breaker each time it starts.
  • Something sharp or metallic has fallen in and you cannot see it.
  • The dishwasher is backing up into the sink at the same time.

If power is involved, leave the unit off until a plumber looks at it. Do not use a plunger in a sink where the disposal is leaking or sparking.

Never put your hand in a disposal

This is the one rule that matters more than any repair. Even a disposal that is switched off can be restarted by someone else flipping the wall switch, and a jammed motor can free itself without warning. Keep hands and children away from the opening.

Safe steps while you wait

Switch the wall switch off, then turn off the breaker for the kitchen circuit, so the unit cannot start. Use a flashlight to look into the drain opening and pull out visible debris only with long tongs, never with fingers. Do not pour drain cleaner into a jammed disposal, because the chemicals sit in the chamber and can splash. If water is leaking, set a pan underneath and call.

Jammed or humming: what is stuck

A disposal that hums but does not spin is jammed. The motor is getting power, but something is stopping the flywheel, and the hum is the motor straining against it. Bones, fruit pits, a bottle cap or a spoon are common culprits.

Most disposals have a hex socket in the center of the bottom, under the sink. With the power off, a plumber uses it to rock the flywheel back and forth and free the jam. After that, the plumber presses the red reset button on the base to restore power.

If the unit does not hum at all, the cause is different. A tripped reset, a failed switch or a dead motor all look the same from above, which is why the plumber checks power before taking anything apart.

Repair or replace the disposal

Many disposals are worth fixing. A jam, a tripped reset or a loose hose connection costs little to correct. Replacement makes more sense when the housing is cracked, the motor will not start after a reset, or the unit has jammed repeatedly.

When you do replace one, tell dispatch which model you have or whether you already bought a new one. The plumber removes the old disposal, fits the new one to the existing flange or installs a new mounting kit, and tests the connections for leaks. That includes reconnecting the dishwasher drain hose, which is a common part of the job.

Disposal or drain line: how to tell

A kitchen sink that backs up into the other bowl is not always the disposal's fault. If the disposal runs freely but water drains slowly, the blockage is probably in the trap or the line beyond it. Grease is the usual cause, and it builds up along the pipe wall until the opening is a fraction of its size.
