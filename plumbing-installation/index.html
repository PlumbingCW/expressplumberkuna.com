Hooking up a house that has no plumbing yet

Some calls come from owners who bought a shell, finished a garage into a living space or built an outbuilding with nothing connected. That is a different job from swapping a faucet. The plumber has to find where the water, drain and vent will tie in, size the lines, and make sure the drain slopes and the fixtures are vented.

Tell dispatch whether the walls and floor are open and whether a rough-in has been done. The answer decides whether this is a one-visit hookup or a staged job with an inspection between steps.

Shutoffs and why every fixture should have one

A fixture without its own shutoff turns a small repair into a house-wide water outage. We install or replace stop valves at sinks, toilets and appliances so you can isolate a single fixture without touching the main. Old valves that no longer close, or that weep at the stem, are replaced during the install because they are the next likely leak.

Know where your main water shutoff is before any project starts. If a job goes wrong and water will not stop, close the main and call. Our burst pipe repair page covers active leaks.

Before the plumber arrives

Clear the work area, measure the space for the fixture, and have the fixture or appliance on site if you bought it yourself. Know where your main shutoff is. If a fixture is already connected, photograph the existing supply and drain so the plumber can see what is behind the cabinet or wall.

Permits and who may pull one

The City of Kuna issues plumbing permits through its Building Department, and the January 2025 application lists new dwellings, plumbing for existing residences and detached shops, water lines, sewer lines and backflow devices among covered work. Not every small job is treated the same, so ask the Building Department at (208) 922-5546 or Permits@KunaID.gov before you start.

Per the City form, work without a permit costs double the permit fee as the minimum charge. DOPL says homeowners may buy a permit only for their own primary or secondary residence and outbuildings. Multi-family permits in Kuna are for contractors only. The adopted code is the 2017 Idaho State Plumbing Code.

A note on sump and ejector pumps

Sump and ejector pump calls come in rarely, and they are different from fixture work. A pump that serves a basement or a below-grade bathroom needs the right discharge path, a check valve and an outlet that is safe for the location. Whether your home has one at all, a plumber confirms on site.

Tell dispatch what the pump serves and what it is doing. If water is standing near outlets, stay out and shut off power only if you can reach the panel safely and dry.
