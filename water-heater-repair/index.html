When a water heater problem is an emergency

A cold shower is miserable but not urgent. These situations are different.

  • Water is leaking from the tank itself, not just from a fitting.
  • The relief valve is discharging steadily or the pipes are banging.
  • You smell gas near a gas water heater. Leave the house and call 911 and Intermountain Gas first, then call us.
  • The unit is in a closet or attic above finished ceilings and water is already dripping through.
  • The house has an infant, an elderly person or a medical need that depends on hot water.

In the first three cases do not wait for the next business day. Shut off what you can reach and call.

The last row is the one homeowners hate. A tank that leaks from its own steel shell has failed, and no repair will stop it. We say that plainly instead of selling a repair that will not hold.

Leaks at the heater: fitting, valve or tank

Water near the heater does not always mean the tank is gone. A plumber traces where the water starts. Dripping from a threaded supply fitting, a connector or the temperature and pressure relief valve can be repaired in a single visit. Water running down the outside of the tank jacket, or wetness under the insulation, points to a failed tank.

Slab and garage installations make this harder because water can travel before it shows. A heater in a garage on the edge of a slab may leak at the tank while the wet spot appears several feet away. If the source is unclear, a leak detection visit can confirm whether the heater or a line near it is the culprit.

No hot water: a short check before you call

Try these only if it is safe

Electric heaters: check the breaker and the reset button on the thermostat cover, but do not open the access panel with wet hands or if the area is damp. Gas heaters: check that the gas valve is on and the pilot or display is lit. If the unit shows an error code, write it down. These checks take a few minutes and give the plumber a head start.

If the breaker trips again right away, stop resetting it. That points to a shorted element, and repeated resets can make the damage worse.

Hard water and sediment in Kuna

The City of Kuna's water quality report says all of southern Idaho has hard water due to naturally occurring calcium. Hard water leaves scale on heating elements and a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On an electric heater the lower element can sit buried in that layer and burn out. On a gas heater the sediment insulates the water from the burner, which wastes fuel and makes the tank rumble.

That is why we flush tank heaters during repair visits when the tank is in good enough shape to justify it. A tank that is already leaking is not flushed, since flushing will not fix a failed shell. If scale is a repeat problem for you, our water softener installation page covers testing your tap and when a softener makes sense.

Gas, electric, hybrid and tankless units

We repair all four kinds. A conventional gas heater needs a safe burner, venting and a gas shutoff within reach. An electric unit needs two working elements, two thermostats and the right breaker.

A hybrid or heat pump water heater is an electric tank with a compressor on top, and it has air filters and condensate lines that a conventional unit does not. Tankless units fail differently, with error codes, scale in the heat exchanger and flow sensors that stop reading. Those need descaling and careful diagnosis, and our tankless water heater page covers them.

Fuel sources in Kuna are Idaho Power for electricity and Intermountain Gas for natural gas, according to the City of Kuna. If you are weighing a fuel change, tell dispatch and we will cover it at a planned visit.
