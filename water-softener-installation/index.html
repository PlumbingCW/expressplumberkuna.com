What scale looks like in the house

Hard water announces itself in ordinary ways long before anyone tests it.

  • White, chalky spots on glass, dishes and shower doors that wipe off and come back.
  • A crust around faucet aerators and showerheads, with weak or sprayed flow.
  • Soap that does not lather well and leaves a film on skin or tile.
  • A water heater that rumbles, pops or runs out of hot water sooner than it used to.
  • Dishwashers and coffee makers that clog or coat over with deposits.
  • Laundry that feels stiff or looks dingy even with plenty of detergent.

One or two of these on their own prove little. Several together make a strong case for testing the water.

Southern Idaho water and the City of Kuna

The City of Kuna's water quality report states plainly that all of southern Idaho has hard water because of naturally occurring calcium. The City supplies drinking water from groundwater wells. The report we reviewed does not publish a numeric hardness figure, so we do not quote one for your street.

That means the useful answer is a measurement at your own tap. A test strip, a store kit or a lab result gives you a number for your house, and that number tells the plumber how large a softener should be. You can also ask the City's Water Department for its latest water quality report.

Use a hardness test strip or kit on cold water, and run the tap for a minute before testing.

3

Taste, smell, rust staining and cloudy water are different problems than hardness.

4

Give dispatch the reading and the symptoms, and a plumber will talk through equipment.

If your results are unusual or you see rust or a rotten-egg smell, do not assume a softener fixes it. Different problems need different equipment, and a plumber should look at the whole setup.

Softener, filter or descaler: what each really does

EquipmentWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Salt-based softenerSwaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium, removing scale causesRemove chlorine, bacteria or most contaminants
Whole-house filterCatches sediment, or taste and odor with carbonSoften hard water
Kitchen reverse osmosisTreats drinking water at one tapProtect the water heater or laundry
Descaler or conditionerClaims to change how scale forms without removing mineralsMatch a softener's results in every home

We are candid about that last row. Independent results on salt-free descalers are mixed, so if scale protection for a heater or tankless unit is the goal, a true softener is the more dependable choice. Salt-free units may suit some homes that want less maintenance, and we say so without overselling.

Before you spend anything

Take a clear glass of tap water, look at it in daylight, and write down what you see on faucets, shower glass and the water heater. Photograph crust around fixtures. Those notes and a water test tell the installer more than any brochure, and they keep you from buying equipment sized for a problem you do not have.

Private wells and testing

Some homes near Kuna are on private wells instead of city water. Central District Health recommends testing well water once a year for bacteria and nitrates, because harmful levels often cannot be detected by taste, sight or smell. CDH does not provide kits, and it lists labs such as Analytical Laboratories and the Idaho Department of Laboratories. Its general line is (208) 375-5211.

A softener does not make unsafe well water safe. Test for bacteria and nitrates first, then discuss hardness and treatment with a plumber. Our water line repair page covers well-to-house piping if the line itself is the concern.

What the installation involves

A softener has to connect to the cold water line, a drain for the regeneration cycle and a power outlet.

The plumber picks a spot, usually near the main shutoff and a floor drain or standpipe, adds a bypass so you can isolate the unit, and sets the control head for your hardness and household.

Where the discharge goes, such as a sewer connection or a septic system, is a decision to make with the plumber, and we do not guess about it.

Water-heated appliances benefit from softened water, but the plumber also asks whether outdoor spigots and irrigation should stay on unsoftened water. Salt and brine are part of the routine, so choose a spot where you can reach the tank to refill it.

Service and repair on softeners you already own

Some calls are not new installs at all. Typical problems include a unit that no longer regenerates, a control head that lost its settings after an outage, a broken bypass valve after a move, or a brine line that has been cut. Lightning and construction work show up in the stories too. A plumber checks the power, the valve, the drain line and the resin tank, and tells you whether repair or replacement makes sense.

If a unit is old and has stopped softening, resin can wear out. We explain what we see before you spend money.

Permits and licensing

The City of Kuna's plumbing permit application lists water conditioning equipment among the work it covers. Rules differ by job, so ask the Building Department at (208) 922-5546 about your install. Without a permit the city's form calls for double permit fees as the minimum charge. Plumbing in Idaho is licensed through DOPL, and the adopted code is the 2017 Idaho State Plumbing Code.
