Many broken lines only show up as a drain that clogs again a few weeks after it was cleared. Either way, the first job is to see inside the pipe before anyone talks about digging.

When a sewer line problem cannot wait

Some warning signs are slow and some are not. These ones mean call now:

  • Sewage rises in a tub, shower or floor drain when you flush or run water.
  • Two or more toilets stop working at the same time.
  • A strong sewer smell appears in the yard, crawlspace or garage.
  • A patch of lawn over the line is sinking, soggy or suddenly greener than the rest.
  • The same clog returns after every cable visit.

Turn off water use in the house and call. A sewage backup carries a health risk, so do not wade through it barefoot and do not run a wet vacuum on it. Gas smell is a different matter, so if you smell natural gas, leave and call 911 or Intermountain Gas before anything else.

How we find out what is wrong with the line

Repairs start with a diagnosis, not a guess. A plumber clears the blockage enough to get a camera in, then follows the pipe from the cleanout toward the street. The video shows cracks, offset joints, sags that hold water, root entry and collapsed sections.

That footage decides the repair, and you can watch it with the plumber. If you only want the inspection on a calmer day, our sewer camera inspection page explains what the visit covers. If the line is simply packed with roots or grease, sewer line cleaning may be all that is needed for now.

Spot repair, lining or replacement

The right fix depends on how much of the pipe is damaged and where it runs. This table shows how the three options usually compare.

OptionBest forTrade-off
Spot repairOne cracked or separated section with the rest of the line soundFixes only the dug section, so a second weak spot elsewhere stays
LiningCracks and root entry along a line that is still round and in placeNeeds a clear, cleaned pipe and cannot fix a collapsed or badly sagging run
Full replacementCollapsed, crushed or heavily sagging pipe or a long run of damageMost digging and the most disruption, but the most complete result

Not every property can use every option. A camera view may show that lining would trap a belly full of water or that a collapsed section has to be dug out. We tell you which options the footage actually supports and why the others do not.

Roots, belly and cracks: what repeat backups usually mean

Roots enter at joints, where a small gap lets in moisture and the root grows into a mat. A belly is a low spot that holds water and waste, which is why a line can drain slowly even when nothing is stuck. Cracks let soil move into the pipe and shift the pipe farther out of line.

Kuna grew from 15,210 residents in 2010 to 24,011 in 2020, so a lot of local sewer lines are not very old. A newer line that backs up repeatedly usually points to a bad joint, a settled trench or roots from landscaping rather than age. That is why we camera the line before quoting a repair.

City main or your own line

Your sewer line runs from the house to the point where it joins the public main. The City of Kuna Wastewater Division operates the mains, and the sewer emergency number is (208) 573-1511. If neighbors on your street are also backing up, call the city first.

If only your house is affected, the problem is almost always in your own line, and the repair is yours to arrange. A camera view can show whether the damage sits on your side of the connection.

Safe steps while you wait for a plumber

Do these first

Stop using water in the house, including the washing machine and dishwasher. Do not pour drain cleaner into a backed-up line, because it will sit in the pipe and then splash on the person who opens it. Keep a path clear to the cleanout if you know where it is. Photograph any wet floors for your insurance records.
