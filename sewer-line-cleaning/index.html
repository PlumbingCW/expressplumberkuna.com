Sewer backup: what to do first

Water has only one way out when the main line is blocked, and it takes the lowest opening it can find. That is why a basement drain, a ground-floor shower or the toilet nearest the main often fills first.

Do these things in order:

  1. Stop using water in the house.
  2. Flush nothing else and run nothing that drains.
  3. Open windows if the smell is strong, and keep people out of the wet area.
  4. Find the cleanout if you know where it is, but do not open it while pressure is behind it.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch what is backing up.

Do not use chemical drain cleaner. It stays in a full pipe and becomes a hazard for the person who opens the line.

Is it the city main or your own line?

The City of Kuna Wastewater Division operates the public sewer mains, and the city sewer emergency line is (208) 573-1511. Your own line runs from the house to the point where it joins that main. Everything on your side is yours to clear or repair.

There is a simple check. If neighbors on your street are backing up too, call the city first. If only your house is affected, the blockage is most likely in your own line, and a plumber with a cable machine and a camera can find out where.

Signs the main line is clogging

A main line rarely fails without warning. The early signs are slow drains in more than one room, a toilet that gurgles when the shower runs, and a drain that clears, then fills again within days.

Gurgling is air being pushed back through traps as water struggles to leave. A bubbling toilet when the washing machine drains is a classic hint that water has nowhere to go. If you see two of these signs together, treat it as a main line problem rather than a one-fixture clog.

How sewer line cleaning is done

The method matches the blockage. Here is how the usual tools compare.

ToolBest forLimit
Cable machineSoft clogs, paper and a first pass to open the lineCan leave grease and root growth on the pipe wall
CameraSeeing what is in the line before and after clearingNeeds the line open enough to pass
Hydro jettingGrease, scale and root growth coating the full pipeNot for pipe that is cracked, collapsed or very fragile

A plumber usually starts with a cable to open the line, then runs a camera to see what caused the blockage. If the camera shows buildup along the wall, a pass of hydro jetting cleans the whole pipe instead of leaving a narrow channel. We explain the choice before work starts.

Roots in the sewer line

Roots find a joint with even a tiny gap, because the pipe carries water and nutrients. Once inside, they thicken and catch paper and waste until the line stops. Cutting the roots clears the line, but they grow back unless the joint is sealed or the pipe is repaired.

That is why a root clog often returns within a year or two. If the camera shows repeated root entry, the conversation shifts from cleaning to sewer line repair, where a damaged joint or section can be sealed, lined or replaced. We would rather show you the footage than sell you a fix you do not need.

Grease, scale and paper

Grease cools in the line and builds a coating that narrows the pipe. Scale from minerals can add to the roughness inside older sections. The City of Kuna water quality report says all of southern Idaho has hard water because of naturally occurring calcium, so a camera look at the pipe wall is worth it when a line keeps slowing.

Wipes labeled flushable are a common cause, too. They do not break down like paper and gather at joints and bends. If the footage shows wipes or a rag wad, we tell you so you can stop the cause.

Safe steps while you wait

Stop all water use. Do not stand in sewage with wet shoes near outlets or cords, and do not mix cleaning chemicals to mask the smell. Take photos of any damage for your records. If you smell natural gas, leave the house and call 911 or Intermountain Gas first.
