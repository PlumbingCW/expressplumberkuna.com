A sewer line is buried, so most problems stay invisible until water stops going down. A sewer camera inspection removes the guesswork by sending a small waterproof camera through the pipe while a plumber watches the screen.

It is a planned visit rather than an emergency one, though the phone is answered 24/7 if you want to book at an odd hour. Sewage coming up in the house is a different situation, and that belongs with our sewer line repair team.

What the camera actually shows

The camera travels from a cleanout or a pulled fixture toward the street. On the screen the plumber can see the pipe wall, the joints and anything sitting in the bottom of the line.

Typical findings include these:

  • Roots pushing into a joint or growing as a mat inside the pipe.
  • Cracks, breaks or a joint that has slipped out of line.
  • A belly, which is a low section that holds water and waste.
  • Grease, scale or debris coating the pipe wall.
  • A collapsed or crushed section where the camera cannot pass.
  • Where the pipe changes material or direction.

A camera shows condition, and it does not replace judgment. A plumber reads the footage, explains what is serious and what is cosmetic, and tells you if the pipe needs more work.

Inspecting before you buy a house

A home inspector usually looks at fixtures, not at the buried line. If you are under contract on a house, a camera look at the sewer line can show whether the seller is leaving you a root problem or a failing section. Ask your agent how your inspection window works, because timelines vary.

Be realistic about the limits. The camera can only see the part of the line it reaches, and a bad finding is something to take back to the seller or your agent, not something we negotiate for you. If it turns up damage, our repair team can explain what the fix would involve before you commit.

When repeat clogs call for a camera

One clog is an event. The same drain clogging every few months is a pattern, and a pattern usually has a cause that cabling will not fix. Roots, a belly or a broken joint can all produce the same slow drain, and they need different answers.

That is the moment to look inside. A camera visit after drain cleaning can show why the line keeps coming back, and it often saves money by pointing at the real problem instead of the symptom. If the footage shows heavy buildup, hydro jetting may be a better next step than another round of cabling.

Reasons to schedule a camera visit

Kuna lines, new and not so new

Kuna went from 15,210 residents in 2010 to 24,011 in 2020, which means plenty of local houses sit on relatively young lines. Young does not mean trouble free, because poor joints, settled trenches and root growth from new landscaping can all affect a newer pipe. A camera visit is useful on a house of any age, and it is the quickest way to tell a clean line from a hidden problem.

Before the plumber arrives

Find your cleanout if you know where it is and clear the area around it. Do not pour drain cleaner into a slow line, since the chemical sits in the pipe where the camera and the plumber have to work. Note which fixtures are slow and when the last backup happened, because that helps the plumber choose where to look.

What a sewer camera inspection costs
