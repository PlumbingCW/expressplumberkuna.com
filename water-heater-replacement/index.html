A failed tank: what to do in the next ten minutes

Close the cold water inlet valve on top of the heater. Switch off the breaker for an electric unit or turn the gas control to off or pilot on a gas one. Open one hot tap in the house to relieve pressure.

Mop up water that is near outlets and move stored items out of the way. Then call. The plumber will want to know the fuel, the approximate size in gallons, where the heater sits, and whether the old unit is on a stand or in a pan.

If you notice a gas smell, leave and call 911 and Intermountain Gas before you call us.

Most replacements finish in a single visit when the unit is a like-for-like swap. A change of fuel or location takes longer and is better planned, and you can read about that in our tankless water heater section.

Safe steps while you wait

Do not touch the heater if water is pooling near an electric unit and you are standing in it. Keep children and pets away from hot surfaces and the relief valve discharge. Never plug a vent or the relief valve. Leave the old tank where it is until the plumber arrives and drains it properly.

Permits and licensing for water heater work in Kuna

The City of Kuna issues plumbing permits through its Building Department. The January 2025 permit application lists water heater replacement among the work it covers, and the form has a field for the contractor license number. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Building Department at (208) 922-5546 or by email at Permits@KunaID.gov about yours. Work done without a permit can cost double the permit fee as the minimum charge, per the city's own form.

Idaho plumbing work is licensed by the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses, and the adopted code is the Idaho State Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code. A homeowner may buy a permit only for work on their own primary or secondary residence, per DOPL, and a homeowner permit is not valid for commercial buildings.

Household size drives tank capacity. A single person and a family of five do not need the same tank. Dispatch will ask how many people use hot water, how many bathrooms you have and whether you run a soaking tub, because those answers set the size.

What comes with a new water heater

Replacement is more than the tank. Plumbers look at the shutoff valves, the flexible connectors, the relief valve discharge line, the drain pan and whether a thermal expansion tank belongs on the system. Whether your system needs an expansion tank depends on how it is set up, so we check rather than assume. Old connectors and corroded shutoffs are replaced during the swap because they are the next likely leak.
