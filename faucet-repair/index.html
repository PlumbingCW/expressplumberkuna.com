When a faucet problem is an emergency

Most drips are slow and can be scheduled. These are the ones to call about right away.

  • Water keeps running from a tub or shower even with the handle turned off.
  • A handle or valve has snapped, stripped or come off in your hand.
  • The shutoff valve under the sink will not close or is leaking itself.
  • Water is spraying from a supply line or from the wall behind a fixture.
  • A leaking fixture is soaking a cabinet, ceiling or floor.

If you cannot stop the water at the fixture, turn off the main valve and call. A cabinet that has been wet for an hour is a much easier repair than one that has been wet all day.

Dripping faucet repair: what is wearing out

Most dripping faucets have one failed part, and the part depends on the style of faucet. A compression faucet with separate hot and cold handles usually needs a new washer or seat. A single-handle faucet usually has a worn cartridge, and a ball or disc style has its own seals and springs.

Sometimes the faucet is too worn to be worth rebuilding. When parts are hard to find or the finish has failed, replacing the whole faucet is the cleaner choice, and we give you both prices side by side.

Bathroom faucet repair and the drain nearby

A bathroom faucet often fails at the aerator, the cartridge or the supply lines under the vanity. Low flow from one side usually means scale or debris in the aerator, and that is a quick cleaning. A leak under the sink is different, and it can come from the faucet connection, the supply line or the drain, so the plumber looks at all three before replacing anything.

If the sink also drains slowly, the leak and the clog may be linked. Our drain cleaning page covers what a clear drain should look like.

A leaky shower faucet or a tub that will not shut off

A shower that drips from the head usually has a failing cartridge or seat in the valve behind the wall. A tub spout that keeps running with the handle off points to the same part. Because the valve is inside the wall, the repair either goes through the handle opening or needs a small access panel.

Stripped or rounded handles are common in older bathrooms. The plumber removes the damaged handle, replaces the cartridge, and fits a new trim kit if the old one will not seal.

If the stop valves for the shower are missing, the main water valve has to be shut off for the repair. Tell dispatch what style of shower you have and where the access is, if you know.

Failed cartridge or stuck shutoff valve

A cartridge fails when its seals wear, when scale locks it in place, or when a handle gets forced and breaks inside. A stuck shutoff valve is a related problem. Valves that have not been turned for years can seize, and they often start leaking at the stem the moment someone tries to close them.

Never force a valve with a wrench if it is already weeping. A plumber can replace the valve, and with a stuck main valve may need the water shut off at the street. That is the point where a call to the City of Kuna Water Department's emergency line at (208) 573-7676 can matter, because staff are on call around the clock for water emergencies.

What to do before the plumber arrives

Safe steps while you wait

Close the shutoff valves under the fixture, turning them clockwise by hand. If they will not close, shut the main valve where the water line enters the house. Open the lowest faucet to drain the pipes and place a bucket under any active drip. Keep water away from outlets and power strips, and photograph the damage for your records.

Do not tape over a spraying leak and leave it. A temporary wrap only slows the water, and pressure builds behind it.

Outdoor spigots and the first freeze

Boise's average first freeze falls in mid-October, according to National Weather Service data cited by KBOI. Outdoor spigots that still have a hose attached are the ones most likely to split when the first hard freeze arrives. Disconnect hoses, drain the line to the spigot, and call us if a spigot drips after the cold snap, since a split body can leak inside the wall without showing outside.
