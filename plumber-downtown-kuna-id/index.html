If you own or rent here, your plumbing question may be about a house, a storefront or a building that is both. Call (208) 679-8357 and tell us which one it is.

Where Downtown Kuna sits

The City of Kuna's long-range plan, called Envision Kuna, treats downtown as its own core area. It names Linder Avenue, West 4th Street and Avalon as possible welcoming entrances, and it proposes widening the downtown core to take in the neighborhood north of 4th Street. The city also adopted a Downtown Revitalization Plan in 2015.

We checked sample addresses on West Main Street, West 4th Street, East Avalon Street and North Locust Avenue against the US Census geocoder, and all of them fall inside the Kuna city limits in Ada County. Kuna City Hall is at 751 West 4th Street.

The plan does not publish a street boundary for downtown, so we do not draw one. If you are unsure whether your address counts as downtown, that does not change how we help you.

Plumbing calls that suit a mixed downtown

Houses near the core and small buildings on the main streets call for different work.

  • Slow or backed-up drains. A kitchen or tub that drains slowly is a drain cleaning job. A floor drain in a shop that keeps backing up may need a larger machine or hydro jetting.
  • Buildings with a business and an apartment. Shared lines can make it hard to tell whose problem it is. Tell us what is on each floor.
  • Hidden leaks. A water bill that jumps in a building with a basement or crawlspace can mean a leak you cannot see. See leak detection.
  • Storefront plumbing. Restroom fixtures, mop sinks and backflow devices fall under commercial plumbing.

Water and sewer in the center of Kuna

The City of Kuna runs the public water and sewer systems. If water is coming up in the street or several neighbors have a backup at the same time, the problem may be on the city side. The city's water emergency line is (208) 573-7676 and its sewer emergency line is (208) 573-1511. If only your building is affected, the problem is usually on your own line.

The City of Kuna's Consumer Confidence Report says southern Idaho water is hard because of naturally occurring calcium. That leaves scale on fixtures and water heaters in every part of town, downtown included. Our water heater repair page explains what scale does to a tank.
