When a clogged drain is an emergency

Most clogs can wait for a scheduled visit. A few cannot, and the signs are easy to spot.

  • Water is rising in a tub or shower when you flush a toilet or run the washer.
  • Sewage is coming up through a floor drain or the lowest fixture in the house.
  • A toilet will not stop overflowing and the shutoff valve behind it does not help.
  • The house has one bathroom and it is unusable.
  • A rental unit is affected and a tenant has called you after hours.

If any of these match, call right away and turn off the water supply to the affected fixture. A sewage backup also carries a health risk, so keep children and pets out of the room until it is cleaned and disinfected.

Kitchen sink clogs

Kitchen drains clog differently from bathroom drains. Grease, coffee grounds, rice and food scraps harden along the pipe wall until the opening is only a fraction of its original size. A plunger moves the blockage around but rarely removes it.

Our technicians run a small cable through the trap arm and into the wall, pull the buildup out, and flush the line with hot water so you can see it running clear. If the sink has a garbage disposal, they check that the disposal is not the restriction, and our garbage disposal repair page covers what happens when the disposal itself has failed.

Snaking, cabling and when jetting is the better tool

A cable machine drives a spinning steel cable through the line. It punches a hole through a clog and scrapes the pipe wall on the way out. For a typical hair clog or a soft blockage that is the right tool and it is quick.

Cabling has limits. A cable can poke a channel through a thick layer of grease or root growth and leave most of the buildup stuck to the pipe wall, so the drain slows again within weeks. When we see that pattern, or when a camera shows heavy coating along the line, we recommend hydro jetting.

Jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the full pipe diameter. We explain the difference before work starts so you pick the method knowing why.

Why drains clog in Kuna homes

The City of Kuna's own water quality report says that all of southern Idaho has hard water because of naturally occurring calcium. Mineral-rich water can leave scale on fixtures and inside pipes over time, and that rough surface gives grease and hair something to grab. That does not mean your water caused the clog, but it is one reason we look at the condition of the pipe and not only the blockage.

Kuna also grew fast. The city went from 15,210 residents in 2010 to 24,011 in 2020, so many drain lines are relatively young. A newer line that keeps clogging usually points to a belly in the pipe, a poor joint, or tree roots from a young landscape reaching a joint, and a sewer camera inspection can show which it is.

What you can try before we arrive

Safe steps while you wait

Stop running water into the clogged drain. Use a plunger only on a single sink, tub or toilet. Do not pour drain cleaner into a drain that is already full of standing water, because the chemicals stay in the pipe and can splash onto the plumber's skin and eyes.

Never mix two different cleaners. If a toilet is overflowing, lift the tank lid, hold the flapper down, and close the shutoff valve at the wall.

A few other habits help between visits. Run hot water for a minute after washing dishes, scrape plates into the trash instead of the sink, and use a hair catcher in showers. Those steps cost nothing and keep small clogs from turning into service calls.
