Cost to Replace a Gas Water Heater in Kuna, ID: What Drives the Price

The price of a gas water heater replacement depends on the unit, the venting, the gas connection and the permit, not just the tank. This guide lists each factor so you can compare quotes fairly.

By the Kuna Express Plumbing teamPublished October 15, 2026
Plumbing problem right now?(208) 679-8357Call Now

Replacing a gas water heater costs more or less depending on five things: the size and type of the new tank, the condition of the venting, the gas line and shutoff, the water connections, and whether a permit applies. No honest number can be given without seeing the setup, so this page covers what moves the price instead of guessing a figure. A plumber who explains each line item is easier to compare than one who gives a single total.

If your tank is leaking, the water supply valve should be closed first and the situation is urgent. If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or Intermountain Gas before you touch anything.

The unit itself

The biggest line item is the heater. Tank capacity, efficiency rating, warranty length and burner design all change what you pay.

A larger family or a home with a soaking tub may need a bigger tank, while a smaller household can often drop a size. Higher efficiency models usually cost more up front and may use less gas over time. Ask what each option changes in practice, because a longer warranty and a higher price often go together.

Venting and combustion air

Gas water heaters exhaust through a vent, and that vent has to match the new unit. Old flues can be undersized, rusted, poorly sloped or routed in a way the new heater does not allow.

Replacing a tank with the same type often reuses the existing vent. Switching to a different style may call for new vent pipe, a different termination or extra combustion air. A plumber checks this on site because a bad vent is a safety problem, not a cosmetic one.

Gas connection and shutoff

The gas line to the heater needs a shutoff, a proper flexible connector or hard pipe, and a leak test after connection. Old connectors and corroded fittings are commonly swapped during a replacement because reusing them risks a leak.

If the gas line is far from the heater or the shutoff is missing, extra pipe and labor raise the cost. Gas work is not a place to improvise, which is another reason the connection is left to a licensed plumber.

Gas smell or leak? Stop here

Leave the house first and do not use switches, phones or flames inside.

- Call 911 or Intermountain Gas from outside.

- Do not try to relight a pilot you suspect is leaking.

- Do not return until the utility or fire crew says it is safe.

- After the area is cleared, call a plumber to inspect the heater and connection.

Water connections, valves and extras

The water side adds its own items. These are the parts that most often show up on a replacement quote:

  • New supply lines or flexible connectors.
  • A shutoff valve if the old one will not close.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe.
  • An expansion tank if the home has a closed system.
  • A drain pan under the heater in a garage, closet or attic.
  • Disposal of the old tank.

Each item is small alone, but together they explain why two quotes for the same tank can differ. Ask which of these are included before you compare totals.

Location and access

A heater in an open garage is faster to swap than one in a tight closet, an attic or a crawlspace. Stairs, narrow doors and tight clearances add time and sometimes a second person.

If the heater sits in a spot that makes future service hard, some homeowners use the replacement to ask about relocation. That is a bigger job and usually changes the venting and gas run, so mention it on the first call.

Repair or replace

Not every failing heater needs replacing. A bad thermostat, igniter or pilot assembly can sometimes be repaired, while a leaking tank cannot. A heater that is old and starting to fail often costs more in repairs than it saves.

If the unit is repairable, water heater repair can fix the part and keep the tank running. If the tank is leaking or the repair would cost close to a new unit, replacement is the better call. A plumber can tell the difference after looking at the age, the leak and the burner.

Hard water also plays a role. The City of Kuna reports that all of southern Idaho has hard water because of naturally occurring calcium. Scale can build up inside a tank and on the burner surface, so ask about flushing a new heater once a year to slow that down.

Gas, permits and your replacement in Kuna

Natural gas in Kuna comes from Intermountain Gas, so that is the utility to contact if you smell gas or have a service question about the meter. Your plumber handles the heater and the pipe from the shutoff to the appliance.

The City of Kuna plumbing permit application, dated January 2025, lists water heater replacement among the work it covers. Call the Kuna Building Department at (208) 922-5546 to confirm what applies to your job. Work done without a permit incurs double permit fees as the minimum charge, so asking first can save money.

Permits that are issued must also start or resume within 180 days or they become invalid, which matters if parts are delayed.

A gas water heater swap has to meet the 2017 Idaho State Plumbing Code, based on the 2015 UPC, which is one reason venting and the relief valve drain are part of the price.
