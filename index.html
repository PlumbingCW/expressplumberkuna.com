The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

What you do before the plumber arrives can limit the damage. These steps work for most leaks and floods.

1

Find the main shutoff

Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. In many homes it is near the water heater, in a garage, or where the line enters the house.

2

Cut power near water

If water is near outlets, appliances or the breaker panel, switch off power to that area only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.

3

Open a faucet low in the house

Draining the lines relieves pressure and slows the leak while you wait.

4

Take photos and call

Photos help with an insurance claim. Then call (208) 679-8357 and describe what you see.

If you smell gas, leave the building and call 911 and your gas utility before you call us. Gas in the Kuna area is supplied by Intermountain Gas.

Plumbing in Kuna: what the water and the growth mean for your pipes

Kuna's drinking water comes from city wells, and the city's own water quality report says that all of southern Idaho has hard water. Hard water is the usual reason water heaters build up scale, faucet aerators clog, and fixtures get crusty. Our water softener installation page covers testing your tap and choosing a softener, and our tankless water heater page explains why descaling matters on hard water.

Growth shapes the other half of the picture. Kuna's population rose from 15,210 in 2010 to 24,011 in 2020, and the 2025 estimate is 31,525. Many homes are young, with systems that are still in their first decades.

Others are on older lots near the original townsite, which the New York Canal opened to irrigation in 1909 and which incorporated as a village in 1915. Ask us to identify your supply pipe material on the first visit so you know what you have.

Irrigation matters here too. The City of Kuna says irrigation season generally begins in mid-to-late April, depending on when the Boise-Kuna and New York irrigation districts deliver water. Spring is when sprinkler line leaks and backflow assemblies get attention. Indian Creek, which runs through the city as a canal fed by the New York Canal, is part of that same system.
