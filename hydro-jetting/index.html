Symptoms that point to jetting, not another cabling

Hydro jetting is a cleaning method, not a repair, so the question is whether your symptoms match the problem it solves. These signs usually do:

  • The same drain or main line clogs again within weeks or months of being cabled.
  • Water drains, but slowly, and the whole house seems sluggish rather than one fixture.
  • Toilets or tubs gurgle when another fixture drains, which suggests a narrowed pipe.
  • A cable went through, but a camera showed grease or scale still coating the wall.
  • A sewer smell returns even though nothing is blocked.

Jetting is the better tool when the pipe is narrowed by buildup rather than plugged by one object. If you have one slow sink, start with drain cleaning instead.

Repeat clogs: what is really going on

A cable pushes a channel through a clog and leaves. The coating along the walls stays, and it catches the next load of paper or grease. That is why the same line can clog every few weeks after cabling.

Roots do something similar. A cable chews through the middle of a root mat, and the rest keeps growing. Jetting uses a nozzle that sprays water in several directions to scour the pipe wall all the way around, so the opening is restored and not just poked through. We explain which problem the camera shows before suggesting it.

Slow main line and gurgling drains

When the main line narrows, every fixture feels it. A shower that drains slowly while the toilet gurgles is air and water competing for the same restricted pipe. This is different from a single clogged sink, where only one fixture misbehaves.

If you see the pattern across the house, the problem is probably in the main. Our sewer line cleaning page covers what happens when sewage is already backing up, and jetting may be the follow-up that clears the buildup behind the immediate blockage.

Jetting compared with cabling

The two tools solve different problems. This table shows when each one fits.

Cable machineA single soft clog or a first pass to open a stopped lineLeaves buildup on the pipe wall, so clogs can return
Hydro jettingGrease, scale and root growth coating the full pipeNeeds sound pipe and access, and is not a repair for damage
CameraSeeing what is in the line before choosing a toolCannot clean anything on its own

Cabling is quick and fine for many clogs. Jetting costs more effort and equipment, so we recommend it when the evidence says a cable will not hold.

When jetting is the wrong choice

Jetting is not right for every pipe. High-pressure water can stress a line that is already weak, and it will not fix a pipe that has already failed.

We hold off on jetting in cases like these:

  • The camera shows cracks, offset joints or a collapsed section.
  • The pipe is very old, brittle or made of a material that has deteriorated.
  • A cleanout is missing and there is no safe way to reach the line.
  • Water would escape through a broken section into the soil.

In those cases the honest fix is a different tool, often a gentler cleaning followed by sewer line repair. A plumber has to see the pipe first, and that is not something we can judge over the phone.

Seeing the pipe before the jet

We camera the line before jetting whenever we can. The footage shows what is coating the wall, whether there are damaged sections and where roots enter. That decides whether jetting is safe and useful.

A sewer camera inspection can also be done on its own if you are not sure the problem is clogging at all. After jetting, a second camera pass confirms the line is open and shows any weak spots the cleaning uncovered.

Safe steps while you wait

Stop running water into a line that is backing up. Skip chemical drain cleaner, which sits in a full pipe and is a hazard to whoever opens it. Keep children and pets away from standing sewage and wet outlets. A gas smell means leave the house and call 911 or Intermountain Gas before you call us.

Hydro jetting in Kuna homes

The City of Kuna water quality report says all of southern Idaho has hard water because of naturally occurring calcium. Over time, minerals can add roughness inside older drain lines, which gives grease and paper more to catch on. That is not the only cause of a stubborn clog, but it is one reason a camera look at the pipe wall is worth doing before jetting.

A newer home can still have a stubborn line. When a young pipe keeps clogging, the cause is often a joint, a sag or a root entry point that jetting alone will not cure.
